Howard Thompson opens a new series on Stoicism, tracing how Marcus Aurelius, Epictetus and Seneca taught mastery over emotion through reason, then draws a striking parallel to Swedenborg's teaching that true freedom only comes once we're led by a love of what is good and true, rather than by our cravings. Paired with it, Rev. Julian Duckworth's \"The Beautiful World of Proverbs\" is a warm, wide-ranging romp through folk wisdom from a dozen cultures, from an Arab saying about doing good and throwing it into the sea to a 1800 BC Assyrian clay tablet, the oldest proverb on record. A short piece on the history of Easter eggs rounds out the issue."
